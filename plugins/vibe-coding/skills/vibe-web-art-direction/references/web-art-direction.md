# Web Art Direction

## Goal

Define an original, coherent visual concept for the actual homepage or landing page, including its opening composition, section rhythm, image language and characteristic details. Make the design recognizable and feasible enough for a visual system and implementation brief to follow.

## Establish the direction

Read the offer, page ledger, accepted brand rules, inspected references and asset inventory. If a useful direction is already specified, develop it rather than producing alternatives by ritual. When the direction is open, compare credible approaches by audience, product meaning, expressive potential, available assets and implementation constraints. Select a direction and state its concrete rationale; do not create an extra approval gate unless the user requested one.

Express the chosen direction through observable relationships: editorial or instrumental type, compact or generous density, light/dark surface structure, image treatment, framing, material, line behavior and motion character. Name the visual idea in project-specific language and explain how it expresses the offer. “Modern”, “premium” and “clean” require these decisions before they can guide implementation.

## Compose the page

1. Design the opening as one composition: header, proposition, supporting text, primary/secondary action and focal visual. Choose a typographic, photographic, demonstrative, layered or interactive hero because it suits this offer. Decide what visitors should notice first and what should invite exploration next. Reserve clear space for reading across the visual's important states.
2. Choose a content-driven hero or an intentionally immersive one. Specify the relation between content, header and viewport rather than declaring every opening `100vh`. Use a minimum height only when useful; content, translated text and short landscape screens must be able to grow the section. Select stable small-viewport or dynamic viewport units according to the intended mobile behavior, and document the short-screen fallback.
3. Sketch each major section's visual role against its job in the page ledger. Change composition, scale, density, media, alignment or interaction when the content merits it. Keep useful repetition inside comparisons, forms and other predictable UI. Inspect the full-page rhythm so several individually attractive sections also make a convincing page together.
4. Design the transitions: continuity of grid or a visual motif, changes of surface, overlap, controlled whitespace, a chapter boundary or a purposeful motion cue. Account for backgrounds, stacking, section entrances and reading order. A transition must not crop content or create arbitrary blank screens.
5. Give header and footer the same level of attention as the central sections. Define their identity, route groups, primary action, alignment, mobile expansion, contact/policy roles and tonal relationship to the page. Use available destinations; no empty link groups or invented business identity.
6. Plan the distinctive graphic work. Use real imagery for real evidence. Give illustrations and diagrams an exact message, composition and style. A 3D object needs a meaningful relation to the product, appropriate lighting/materials, a camera/composition and a simpler display when interaction is unavailable. Use iconic or ornamental shapes when their form is intentional rather than as a substitute for understanding the offer.

## Materials and detail

Specify where a gradient, glass surface, shadow, grain, line field or volume adds depth or directs attention. Define color stops, light direction, opacity, edge, scale and the background it depends on. Glass needs readable content over changing backgrounds and a solid fallback. A gradient should preserve hierarchy through all its stops; adding several unrelated hues is not a visual system.

For algorithmic graphics, choose the field's meaning and composition first, then its geometry and motion. A contour may echo a material or landscape; a grid may express precision; a flow may suggest a process. Expose palette, scale/density, seed and speed as design parameters. Reserve quiet areas, cap visual complexity and plan a static form. Use the effect in the sections that benefit from it rather than turning the entire site into a moving surface.

Set the finish language: icon family, stroke weights, borders/dividers, corner logic, image crops, masks, highlights and focus/hover character. Align small details to the page's main visual idea. The [visual-design principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/) are useful for judging hierarchy and relationships; the chosen expression remains specific to this project.

## Completion and handoff

Return a direction with an opening composition, a section-by-section composition map, transition/material rules, asset production briefs and responsive adaptations. Include enough concrete dimensions or sketches to communicate scale and hierarchy, while leaving exact shared values for **Web Visual System**. A capable builder should be able to distinguish this direction from a generic page and explain how it serves the actual offer.

If visual tools are available and the task benefits, produce a focused styleboard or opening/section study. Inspect it before describing its appearance as successful. A text-only direction remains a proposal; identify the visual decisions still requiring a rendered check.
