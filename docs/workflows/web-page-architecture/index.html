Goal

Translate an offer brief into a complete page narrative and functional section plan for a homepage or landing page. Specify what each section earns in the visitor's decision, what content it needs and how it behaves. The result is the information and conversion architecture for the design stage, not a mandatory list of website blocks.

Starting context

Use the current offer, audience, proof, action destinations and brand constraints. For an existing site, inspect its actual page and linked funnel before proposing a new order. Preserve accepted requirements and reusable working modules. If the offer is materially undefined, obtain Web Offer Synthesis; do not interrupt a well-defined request merely to repeat that stage.

Build the narrative

Map the visitor's progression from orientation to understanding, trust, evaluation and action. A returning customer or high-intent campaign visitor may need a shorter path. A homepage serving several audiences needs recognizable routes to the right offering. State the organizing principle and which questions belong on this page.

Create a section ledger with these fields: section/job, user question, concrete message, proof or asset, interaction/CTA, destination, composition opportunity, and required data. Order the sections so a claim meets its explanation or proof near the point it is needed. Identify important transitions and changes of pace. Consolidate sections that repeat the same job; expose a missing section when a conversion depends on an unanswered question.

Use the following modules only when their purpose fits the offer:

Visitor need Candidate module Information or behavior needed
Recognize the project and find a path Header and navigation Identity, real destinations, primary action, mobile disclosure and current-location behavior
Understand the offer immediately Hero Audience-relevant proposition, supporting explanation, action and a credible focal visual or demonstration
Understand how the result happens Product demonstration, process, before/after or explainer Real states or a clearly described illustration; useful labels, controls and an accessible equivalent
Evaluate fit or advantages Use cases, benefits, specification or comparison Distinct decision criteria with examples; comparable bases and meaningful limitations
Believe an important claim Review, case study, customer story or social proof Attribution, scope, permission/source and the result actually evidenced
Understand cost or value Tariffs, comparison or calculator Billing basis, included limits, real calculation assumptions, meaningful inputs and a reachable next action
Try or select an option Configurator, quiz, selector or interactive preview State model, valid choices, resulting recommendation and a non-gesture control path
Reduce uncertainty before committing FAQ, guarantee or practical information Real policies and answers to actual objections; no invented promises
Receive something useful Lead magnet, signup, enquiry or booking form Deliverable, minimal fields, recipient/delivery path, success, validation, failure and recovery
Find or visit a place Address, service area, map or appointment module Confirmed location/coverage, hours when relevant, directions and a textual alternative
Understand a time-bound offer Promo or campaign module Actual eligibility, dates, terms and destination; no fake scarcity or resetting countdown
Navigate after reading and verify identity Footer Useful grouped routes, contact/identity, applicable policy links and real social destinations

Include other sections when the product needs them. A gallery, timeline or editorial story should reveal something visitors cannot learn from another feature list. A slider must serve a real collection or comparison; content importance and discoverability may favor visible items or manual progression.

When the first screen can do the work

For a tool a visitor can try immediately, sketch the opening around one useful product action. A visitor might describe a task, configure a shelf or estimate a quantity. Reuse an existing product control where its behavior fits. Specify what input survives sign-in or navigation, where the visitor resumes, which controls are previews, and when a real effect starts. Walk the journey with one distinctive input and check that the destination receives it exactly once. Keep supporting sections that answer a real decision on this path.

Plan variation and conversion

Give major sections a content-appropriate composition: an editorial opening, a product stage, a precise comparison, a full-width evidence story, an interactive instrument or a compact decision band. Keep their shared grid, type and color logic identifiable. Changing a background color does not by itself change composition. Repeated UI such as pricing options should retain the repetition that makes comparison easier.

Mark the action opportunities at relevant decision moments. Specify whether each is navigation, disclosure, download, calculation, lead submission or transaction. Distinguish a real integrated action from a proposed integration that still needs configuration. Do not label a simulated form or calculator as live.

Completion and handoff

Review the full reading path against the offer: the opening is clear, essential claims have evidence, required objections are answered, and every important action has a destination or an explicit integration gate. Remove ornamental sections that add scrolling without adding understanding, proof, utility or feeling appropriate to the brand.

Return the ordered section ledger, header/footer route groups, content and asset requirements, conversion paths and unresolved data/integration decisions. Pass it to Web Art Direction. Planning stops before implementation unless the user separately requested a build. The NN/g homepage principles provide a useful check on orientation and actionable navigation; the page's actual audience determines its architecture.
